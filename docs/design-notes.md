# Frac-Ground design notes

## Chosen direction

The tutorial borrows the clear, short-step pacing the learner likes in Duolingo: a visible sense of where they are, one small task at a time, immediate feedback, and a steady forward action. It does not reuse Duolingo characters, wording, or artwork. The original tutor is a small peach geometric face called Mika, paired with on-screen text. The learner can focus on the maths without a large character taking over the screen.

The palette is near-black, warm white, and light peach. Peach marks the current step and tutor; black anchors the main actions and example; white keeps the work area open. Success and error messages include words and icons as well as color. The product is named **Frac-Ground**, with a compact lowercase **fg** mark.

## Flow and teaching choices

- **Explain:** four short concept cards explain why denominators need to match, how to find a common denominator, how to rename fractions, and how to add and simplify.
- **Watch:** the example `1/2 + 1/4` unfolds in four lines, with commentary shown as each line appears.
- **Together:** the learner types each line for `1/3 + 1/2` and gets a specific response before moving on.
- **Solo:** the learner types each line for `3/4 + 1/6`, with hints still available.

The next action appears only after the learner checks a line correctly. A learner can go back, restart, or open a hint. Feedback identifies common errors such as adding denominators or changing a denominator without scaling the numerator. An equivalent but unsimplified answer gets a nudge to simplify.

On a phone the section rail becomes a compact progress bar, keeping more room for the current line and the on-screen keyboard. Fraction entry uses familiar `n/d` text; the `a/b` button inserts a slash without requiring symbol hunting. On a laptop the persistent rail shows the lesson map.
